            Before you cut

            This is a starting design, not engineering. It holds people. The members carrying them are the three rails, the three legs, and the six bolts at the foot of the back. The load numbers above are estimates. Load it gradually the first time: sit, then lean, then two of you.

            Read the stamp on each pallet. HT means heat treated and is fine to sit on. MB means fumigated with methyl bromide: do not use that one for furniture.

            Pallet deck boards are thin. They carry you sitting, spread across two or three boards. Do not stand on one.

            Drawn from your photo, so check with a tape: where the boards sit on the back pallet, the board width (drawn at 5 1/2 in), and that the seat pallet has bottom boards under its ends and middle. Deck thickness and stringer height have sliders above; set them to what your tape says.

            The step that goes wrong: building three identical frames. The right one is a mirror. If its leg is on the wrong face you will find out when the back pallet will not sit against the rail. Dry-fit before the glue.

            What this build teaches: the lap joint, and cutting repeated angles against a stop. You practise both on the first frame. The next step up is the half lap, where each piece is notched to half its thickness so the whole frame sits in one plane, the way the cedar chair in your reference does.
